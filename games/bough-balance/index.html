How balance works

Every beam weighs one unit, centred at its pivot. For each hanging object, multiply its weight by its signed arm distance: left is negative, right positive. A beam balances when these pulls add to zero. A beam’s own centred weight creates no pull on itself, but counts in the weight felt by its parent. The diagram keeps beams level for reading; the meters show their actual imbalance.

Every bird has a letter and weight. Choose it and a numbered hook, then inspect the exact forecast before hanging it. A bird already on another hook swaps with the selected hook’s occupant. A bird from the tray displaces the current occupant back to the tray. Return to tray removes just the selected hook’s bird. Nested beams are fixed and cannot be moved.

There is no clock or failure penalty. Undo and restart are always available, including after success; records survive. All hooks must be filled and every beam balanced to finish. Replay for fewer actions. The 200-action save limit is a storage bound, not a target: undo or restart if reached.

Tab/Enter/Space operate all controls. On the page or bird/hook buttons: 1–6 selects a bird, arrows select a hook, H hangs, R returns a bird to the tray, Z undoes. Other controls keep normal keys. Pause stops input.
