Reefwright currently has the strongest recorded enjoyment/polish assessment. Copper Cadence supplies unusual machinery and sound; Wellward supplies the clearest bank-and-upgrade loop. These are provisional reasons to keep them in contention, not publication decisions. Ratchet Run should earn a place through responsive action and replay rather than being included merely to fill a genre slot.

Salt & Sail shares a nautical setting with Lanternwake and cargo imagery with Counterweight/Ratchet Run, but its player decisions differ: fit shaped goods into a hold, choose two lashings, then compensate for that exact load while continuously steering and reefing. Reefwright's polyomino placement instead changes ecology and open-water scoring. Those mechanical distinctions justify a separate candidate; they do not justify publishing every thematically similar game.

Pocket Palaeo combines tactile soil removal with load distributed over a connected fossil graph. Braces, lifting point and heavy bones determine damage; its museum seals buy faster brushing, an extra support and more resin. This differs from Wellward’s continuously swinging winch and from first-batch crafting/cleaning loops. Its most valuable improvement is the exact support-risk preview; native browser checks also exposed touch input problems that the engine-only campaign could not catch. The fixed excavation layouts limit discovery after the first recovery, so it must compete on readable manual work and collection rather than implied long-term depth.

## Remaining evaluation work

- All twenty initial candidates are now built and improved. Complete the eleven remaining additional-round rendered reviews before final selection.
- Revisit the strongest candidates after the full slate exists. Prioritize meaningful progression and readable controls over adding arbitrary counts or waiting time.
- Compare finalists by enjoyment, depth, polish, reliability and variety, with explicit limitations. Automated winning routes establish reachability and regression coverage, not human enjoyment or hours of content.
- Select a small complementary group, integrate against freshly read main, update catalogue/launch checks for the current shell, and test the actual release. Preserve the first batch and all unrelated work.

Static Orchard uses continuous aim, charge/release timing, enemy motion, armor, shield windows and emergency pulse range. Afterlight’s Lumen Grove uses static chain placement; Orchard Atelier catches falling harvest. The shared orchard/electric imagery does not replace those distinct player actions. The first pulse design cleared the whole screen too cheaply; restricting it to the selected fruit’s vicinity gives it an emergency role and makes fruit selection consequential. Its compact progression earns a place in the comparison, not automatic publication.

Borrowed Gravity’s central decision is where to borrow, swap and return a pull while collecting samples. It is not a reskin of Afterlight’s Moonwell projectile launches or Wellward’s swinging winch. Its preview is deliberately a no-input forecast: the player still steers around scanners. Jump buffering and ledge grace improve forgiveness without changing the original campaign routes. The four authored galleries are especially compact, so it is a weaker depth contender; its controlled visual identity and gravity traversal must earn any eventual catalogue place.

Tea for Ghosts asks players to interpret a visitor’s request and correct floral, body, sweet and roast balance from tasting feedback. Temperature affects both extraction and the visitor’s preferred warmth; steeping and dilution are meaningful recipe choices. This differs from the first batch’s ingredient-growing/cooking loop and the action or spatial-planning games in this batch. Exact previews and one-step undo make recipe experiments easier to understand. Its fixed cast and small equipment cabinet limit lasting depth, so it must compete on clarity and character rather than implied scale.

Murmuration controls the spacing of a whole flock rather than a single vessel or character. Split wings can gather both sides of a reed island, while a conservative gathered wing can take one side and sacrifice a food feather. A wider detour preserves birds but can leave the roost underfed. These verified alternative outcomes add a small risk/reward choice. The four migrations share the same two obstacle types, so their changing layouts and crosswinds are variation within one game, not separate candidates or evidence of a long campaign. Its depth assessment should remain modest during final selection.

Night Clerk shares a ghost-hospitality setting with Tea for Ghosts, but its decisions operate on a room adjacency graph rather than recipe composition. Moving one noisy guest can disturb several others; a felt screen silences incoming noise but cannot change a physical near/away requirement. The last three shifts have no successful allocation without a screen, verified by exhaustive unpadded room permutations. Five stages and a single kit keep the depth rating modest. The two games should compete for catalogue space despite their mechanical distinction.

Clockwork Tide’s player controls water routing, gate opening, gear ratio and hopper load. Water stored during the flood is released against the lower sea on the ebb; the changing head generates energy, while product speed/torque windows decide whether a load succeeds. Exhaustive first-commission state search shows that bypass filling cannot be skipped. The manual brake rescues reachable overspeed states; the governor automates trimming but cannot supply torque. Copper Cadence instead routes conserved beads through unequal tracks and gates. Both should compete on clarity and meaningful machinery rather than receive two catalogue slots merely because their themes differ.

## Ember Parcel additional-round review — 28 October

Recoverable boardwalks turn one bundle into a temporary safe crossing and later fuel, while lifting restores water cost or garden exposure. The choice is distinct from the other slate's continuous winch/cargo movement. All four kept and all four reclaimed routes finish with no burned gardens; on those fixtures, recovery adds two actions and three arrival warmth, improving score by one. A return trip can make leaving the boardwalk preferable. Original shortest routes may still outperform construction: do not mistake an extra mechanic for a deeper campaign. Four short fixed maps and no sound layer remain limitations. Provisional enjoyment 3, depth 2.5, polish 3.5, reliability 4.5, variety 4 out of 5; this is agent judgment, not a player study. Retain for comparison, not automatic catalogue selection.
