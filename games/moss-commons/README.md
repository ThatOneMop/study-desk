# Moss Commons

Third collection slot 52. Original compact settlement-planning prototype. Spend limited timber on connected houses, gardens, orchards, wells and paths. Houses create population demand; wells supply water and boost adjacent gardens; fertile land offers another way to meet food requirements. Win by supporting at least three houses with enough food and water for all residents. No timers, idle resource generation or arbitrary production quotas.

Three small authored sites vary rocks, fertile plots and timber budgets (20/19/18). A demonstrated seven-building layout costs 17 timber at each site; an alternative second-site route spends 18, uses a path and meets the food requirement with one unwatered fertile garden. Orchards add a competing land-use strategy: each costs 3 timber and supplies 6 food while two orthogonal neighbours remain empty buildable meadow, dropping to 1 food if crowded. Rocks and map edges cannot satisfy that requirement; wells and fertile ground do not change orchard yield. New six-building orchard routes complete every site for 16 timber, one less than the demonstrated irrigated-garden routes. These are validation examples, not claims of optimal strategy or lengthy content. Unlimited undo and retry support experimentation; records track timber remaining after a win. Additional houses increase requirements rather than creating free points.

Original CSS village artwork and native plot buttons/select controls. Minimum 44px targets, visible focus, text labels independent of art, projected resource totals, explicit warnings before crowding an existing orchard, individual orchard yield/open-neighbour labels and invalid-placement explanations are implemented. Keyboard uses native Tab/Enter/Space/select behavior. Actual rendering, native activation, touch and mobile layout remain unverified. Pause/dialog and same-origin parent visibility guards prevent construction while inactive.

Only `study-desk-moss-commons-v1` is used. The save stores site, bounded construction journal and local records. Decoder replays connectivity, costs, occupied plots, rocks, completion and unlock consistency. Corrupt data stays untouched until confirmed recovery/reset. Storage failures allow a temporary session. Local records are not authenticated scores.

## Relocation refinement

An optional Relocate action moves an existing building or path to an empty buildable plot for one timber. The hall cannot move. A full connectivity check requires every occupied plot to remain linked to the hall after the move, including branches left behind; simply touching one neighbour at the destination is insufficient. Food recalculates from the final layout, so moving a house away from an orchard can restore open meadow, while moving a garden or well can change fertility/irrigation benefits. This is a planning trade-off: preserve later construction at a timber cost, or undo for a full refund and rebuild those later choices.

Choose Relocate, pick the source in a labelled native selector, select the destination plot, inspect resource totals, then commit. Selection does not change the saved settlement. Undo restores the original location and timber; retries and records keep their existing behavior. Exact new journal entries are `{kind:'move',from,p}`. Existing construction journals and saves retain their shapes and outcomes. Terminal sites cannot be edited without undo/retry, as before. Since every action costs at least one timber and starting supplies are at most twenty, the existing journal bound remains sufficient.

19 tests pass (12 engine, 7 jsdom 26.1.0), including all previous fourteen checks. New tests cover crowded-orchard recovery without deleting later buildings, full connectivity, invalid sources/destinations/actions/budgets, all three relocation campaigns with per-action reload, exact old-save preservation, preview/commit separation, pause, undo, completion/retry/records and save isolation. Both scripts pass syntax checks. Demonstrated relocation campaigns finish with 3/2/1 timber; moving is not a claimed globally optimal strategy.

## Neighbourhood challenge

Completing a standard site now unlocks an optional neighbourhood redesign on that site. Before, food and water were pooled across the whole connected settlement and housing position only affected access and orchard space. In this mode, every house must additionally share an edge with a well and a garden or orchard. One producer can serve several neighbouring homes; diagonals and paths do not relay service. Global food/water capacity still matters, so a crowded orchard can meet a local-access requirement while failing to produce enough for everyone.

Existing terrain, timber, building costs and standard goals are unchanged. The combination creates new placement trade-offs: combine a high-yield orchard with gardens to reach homes, share wells, and use relocation to repair access without forgetting that irrigation and meadow space affect capacity. Fixtures demonstrate completion of all three challenges with 2/0/0 timber left; these are feasible routes, not optimality or enjoyment claims. The second fixture uses a connecting path. Three compact sites remain three sites, not newly counted games.

The challenge button requires a standard completion on the current site. Mode changes confirm restarting the layout; both record sets remain. Retry keeps the mode. Site navigation returns to standard mode. Plot labels and forecasts identify homes missing food or water access, including changes caused by relocation, before committing. There is no new key binding: native buttons/selects retain input ownership.

The v1 key and legacy save structure are preserved. Only challenge users gain optional `neighbourhood: true` and `neighbourhoodRecords` fields. Standard records still unlock sites; independent challenge records track timber left. Decoding validates mode, per-site unlocks, bounded records, journal replay and terminal completion. Every action still costs at least one timber, so the existing twenty-action bound remains sufficient. No extra supplies are granted.
