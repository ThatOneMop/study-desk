THE LITTLE FLIGHT COMPUTER

Beepline

Write a repeating flight program. Visit every flower, then return to the hive. Fewer commands make a tidier dance.

Your loop

Starts facing east →. Commands repeat from the beginning. Editing rewinds the bee; your records stay.

Flight handbook

F — Forward: fly one square; hitting a hedge ends the flight. G — Glide: fly until the square before a hedge, collecting flowers along the way. L / R: turn left or right without moving. S — Scout: if the next square is a hedge, turn right; otherwise fly one square. It reacts each time the loop reaches it.

Try replacing a Forward that hits a hedge with Scout: the same slot can now move or turn depending on the bee’s surroundings. Scout always turns right at a hedge; it never glides. Use up to six commands. All turns are relative to the bee’s current direction. Step follows a glide one square at a time so you can inspect its route. The highlighted command is the next one to execute, or the glide currently in progress.

Pollen delivery challenge: unlock it by completing a normal garden. Carry at most two flowers’ pollen at once. Fly through the hive to unload; reaching a third uncollected flower with a full bag ends the attempt. Already-collected flowers are safe. Your program keeps its place in the loop when unloading. Deliver all three to finish. Delivery records are separate; changing mode rewinds but keeps your program and records.

Visit each pink flower and return home to unlock the next garden. Back one step lets you retrace the flight, including a crash, loop or completed route, without losing earned records. Step forward again to compare. Rewind and edit as often as you like. A repeating state means this exact program cannot finish. There is no clock. Space steps when focus is on the board or page; form controls use their normal keys. Run flight animates the same steps and can be stopped.
