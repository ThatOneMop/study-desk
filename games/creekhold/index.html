How to play

Build a river colony of nibs. Keep them watered, fed and housed, and store enough water to get through every dry spell.

Your goal

There is no timer and no final boss. Your aim is to keep the colony alive and growing through each dry spell and sour tide. The Next card at the top left gives you nine goals in order, from your first pump to building up high. Marks (in the Menu) are badges for things like surviving your first dry spell or raising twelve nibs.

The game ends only if every nib is gone. You can always try the same river again.

Controls

Mouse

  • Pick a group (Paths, Water…) in the bottom bar, then a building.
  • Left click a tile to place it. With Footpath, Stick dam, Clay levee or Remove, drag to paint a line.
  • Right-drag to move the map. Scroll to zoom. Hover a tile or button for a tip.
  • Click a building with nothing picked to see its crew, pause it or take it down.

Keyboard

  • Space start or pause time · W A S D or arrows move the map · Q / E turn the view
  • 1–7 pick a build group · X remove tool · T Idea tree · H this page
  • V water depth · R path reach · G power · C sour water
  • Esc drops the picked building, or opens the Menu.

Touch

  • Tap a group, then a building, then tap a tile to place it.
  • Drag one finger to move the map, pinch to zoom. Touch and hold, then drag, to paint paths or dams.
  • The View button cycles the water, reach, power and sour views. ? opens this page.
Resources (the top bar)
  • Nibs — your colony. Adults work; kits grow up after a few days.
  • Mood — how happy nibs are, out of 100. Happy nibs work and walk faster, and new kits only come when the colony is content.
  • Water — stored drinking water / how much you can store. Pumps fill it; tanks make it bigger.
  • Food — berries, carrots, tubers and cooked meals all added up.
  • Logs — chopped by a timber mark. Almost every building costs logs.
  • Planks — made from logs at a saw. Needed for bigger buildings.
  • Gears — pressed from planks with power. Needed for the most advanced works.
  • Ideas — earned at a wonder desk. Spend them in the Idea tree to unlock new buildings.
Seasons: clear water, dry spells and sour tides

The Season chip shows what the river is doing and how many days are left.

  • Clear water — the river flows. Pump and store as much as you can.
  • Dry spell (orange chip) — the river stops and slowly drains away. Nibs drink only from your tanks, plus whatever a pump can still draw from the pool behind your dam. Each dry spell is a little longer than the last.
  • Sour tide — later on, the river sometimes turns brown and sour. Pumps will not drink it and nibs who wade in it get sick. Shut sluice gates to keep it away from your pool and fields.

You get a warning about a day before each change. Build tanks and a dam before the first dry spell. On Gentle it starts on day 7; harder tempers bring it sooner.

Keeping nibs happy and alive
  • Thirst is the big danger. A nib with no water for most of a day will die. Keep Water above zero!
  • Hunger is slower, but a nib with no food for about a day will also die.
  • Beds — a nib with no bed sleeps rough at the hearth and is grumpy.
  • Sour water makes nibs sick: slower, and much less happy, until it wears off.
  • Nibs get happier from a full belly and drink, good sleep, a bed, trying different foods (carrots, tubers, meals), comfort buildings nearby, and time at a story fire or splash pool.
  • Nibs grow old after many days. New kits keep the colony going: Reed Circle kits arrive when a bed, food and water are all spare; Cog Lodge kits come from a berry vat.
Buildings

Grey buttons in the dock still need to be learned in the Idea tree. Most buildings need a free nib to work there. Everything must touch a footpath that leads back to the hearth.
