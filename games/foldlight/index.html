How folding works

Choose which side of the crease moves. Left and right folds use a vertical crease between columns. Top and bottom folds use a horizontal crease between rows. The chosen side reflects across that line and lands on top of any paper already there. Choose Tuck underneath to slide the turned stack below the stationary layers instead, preserving their top colour. An empty destination gets the same turned stack either way. Both choices cost one fold.

Whole-sheet turnover costs one move: it mirrors all panes left-to-right, reverses every stack and flips every face, without merging panes. Select its checkbox to preview or commit with the same controls. Turnover ignores crease and tuck-under settings.
Every moving stack turns over: its layer order reverses and each piece shows its other face. Both faces have different printed colours. Panes show their top colour and total layer count. Tap a pane to inspect its layers from top to bottom.

A fold must move some paper and keep all of it inside the six-by-six frame. Empty space may be folded over; it contributes no paper. Match every target pane, including empty ones, within the pattern’s move allowance. There is no clock. Undo works after a finished or missed pattern; retry keeps your fewest-move records.

Tab/Enter/Space operate every control. On the page or paper panes, F commits, T toggles whole-sheet turnover, U toggles underneath, V toggles the preview and Z undoes. These shortcuts leave other controls alone. Pause stops input.
