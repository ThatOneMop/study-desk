# Game quality

Checklist for every catalogue game. A pass means the item is present in the current build. Scores are from this round’s code review and a 1400×816 opening check, not a full playthrough of all 64 games.

The quality bar from playtesting is **Little Lake**, **After Hours**, **Forge Arena**, and **Roof Runner** (7/10). Match that bar: the first screen is the title or the playfield, and Restart is never where the first click lands.

## Checklist

- **Opening.** A title screen or the playfield. Not a wall of instructions, and not a Retry button in the header or under the first click.
- **Pause.** A pause control.
- **Goals.** The current aim is visible.
- **Hints.** Help or an on-screen hint can be opened.
- **Feedback.** A hit, collect, or miss has sound, shake, or a visible burst.
- **Curve.** The first stage is gentler than the later ones, or playtesting already called the game fair.
- **Saves.** Progress is stored in the browser.
- **Best.** A best score, medal, or route is kept.
- **Art.** The game has its own illustrated UI or canvas.
- **Keys.** Keyboard play or keyboard shortcuts.
- **Touch.** Buttons or pointer input that work without a keyboard.
- **Copy.** No “development prototype”, “not selected”, or “candidate” labels.

## Notes from this round

- Instruction panels start closed. Tidepost’s sailing notes start hidden behind How to sail. Roof Runner still shows its short guide under the city, which is the pattern the other games are measured against.
- Restart moved out of the header in Deep Pocket, Inkline, Glasshouse, Stone Song, and Last Match. In the ten Afterlight games it sits in a closed Chapter menu and asks before it resets the chapter.
- Little Foundry, Pocket Expedition, Circuit Garden, Spore Kitchen, Stone Song, Storm Signal, Moss Market, and Glasshouse use a tighter first screen so the board, beds, or stall is in view.
- Lanternwake, Paper Odyssey, and Reefwright gained a title gate, pause, sound, shake, and particles, and an easier first stage.
- Tiny Wilds’ first reel is wider and slower. Afterlight and Nightshift use a shorter first chapter on screens at most 600px wide, and their embedded Back button returns to the Study Desk catalogue.
- Forge Arena uses the 3D battle inside the Study Desk iframe when WebGL is available.
- Tiny Wilds has a title screen, sound with a mute toggle, and a Stardew-style vertical reel. Hold to lift the bar, and fish dart, sink or float. A catch the fish never leaves pays ×1.5, and treasure chests appear once you know two species. The waters are redrawn as cached pixel art with a crisp bitmap font, and catches leap to the angler with particles, ripples and shake.
- Lost & Found plays more like a metal detector. It has a target-ID and depth screen, a high or low tone by ID, junk targets with a filter (Q), a SPACE pinpointer, and digging one stroke at a time with a reveal card. It also gained a title screen, pause (P/Esc), sea ambience, and painted coasts. Later coasts bury finds deeper and add more junk.
- Lost & Found on the Moon has a glowing tether with a winch hum, low-gravity hops and dust, and Earth in the sky. Hauls of several items earn up to ×2 credits, and work orders pay +50%. It shows a best-haul record, and the first sector is closer in and lighter.
- Autumn Drift plays closer to Leaf Blower Revolution. Click to pick, or hold and drag to blow leaves off the lawn or into the leaf basket for double acorns. Quick sweeps build a combo of up to +50%, and heavier leaves need more strength to move. The garden is redrawn as top-down pixel art (mown lawn, pixel trees, fence, bench, basket). It adds a blower hum and pick pops with sound on by default, a How to play panel (?), and smaller first areas (36, 48 and 58 leaves).
- Underleaf leans toward SimAnt and The Ant Game. It has pixel side-view soil in three layers (topsoil, red subsoil, clay) with roots, pebbles and glowing fungus, plus pixel ants. Brood shows egg, larva and pupa, new chambers dig out with dirt bursts, and jelly beans or apple slices fall from the oak for bonus food. The first broods hatch in 4 seconds. Shortcuts are 1–4, F, D, J, P and the arrows, scoped to the page and the colony so typing elsewhere is untouched.
- Tidepost takes Dredge’s mood. It has a pixel-art chart with fog, dusk and night palettes on later charts and a lantern glow, a ferry sprite with a wake, and animated currents and squalls. A Dredge-style hold grid shows mail slots and hull plates that crack in squalls. It adds a title screen, pause (P), sound, shake and splashes, and a harbour guide with a looser par on the first chart.
- Lost & Found, Lost & Found on the Moon, Autumn Drift, Underleaf, and Tidepost are covered by `tests/game-smoke-browser.cjs`: each boots without page errors, and its title screen leads to play.
- Little Lake now opens on a pixel title card and keeps records like Stardew Valley's fish collection: each species stores its heaviest catch, the save keeps your best catch by value, and the journal has a Records filter. A catch shows a pixel card with New species, New record, Best catch and Legendary stamps. A Perfect reel, where the marker never leaves the green after the first third, pays +25%.
- Lucky Company's floor targets now climb like Balatro antes: 90, 135, 175, 220, 265, 315, 370, 430, 495 and 565, instead of a flat 140 + 35 per floor. The Lobby is gentler and Cloud Nine is harder. A Best run badge keeps your best floor, wins and peak chips on this device, and a chime and banner mark a new best floor.
- Forge Arena battles now have a pause button and P/Esc keys, and they pause automatically when Study Desk hides the game. The pause menu shows kills, damage and time left, with Resume and Leave battle. Hits show a marker at the crosshair and damage numbers; kills show a DESTROYED banner with a boom and screen shake; taking damage flashes a red vignette with a thud. This is modelled on War Robots and Mech Arena hit feedback.
- Tea for Ghosts leans toward Coffee Talk. It opens on a title card (Open the tearoom, Enter) and visitors speak in a typewriter speech bubble that reacts to each cup, with a face that smiles, hesitates or shivers. Each visitor has their own tint and accessory. Brewing tilts in a teapot and pours the blend's own colour, and a restored memory gets sparkles, a glow and a banner. Synthesised sound covers scoops, pouring, a bell, chimes, and rain with a soft music box, with a Sound toggle. Rest (P/Esc) pauses, and it pauses itself when the tab is hidden. Keys: 1–6 add a scoop, Shift+number removes, T/S/W change the settings, B brews, O offers, Z undoes, R recalls, A pours another cup, N welcomes the next visitor. The first visitor's memory returns at 70 and the second at 75, then 80 as before.
- Counterweight now plays like a polished Steam puzzle game. It opens on a Clock in title card, and P/Esc or the Pause button starts a Tea break that also kicks in when the tab is hidden. Every action has a synthesised sound over a soft harbour ambience (with a sound toggle). Lifts show HOOKED, SOFT LANDING, DAMAGED and OVERLOAD callouts, with dust, a shake and a red alarm flash, and a finished job throws confetti. The first job is coached by the Foreman, whose tip names the next move from a solver that can finish all five jobs under par; H or the Tip button asks for it on any job. Saves and records are unchanged.
- Round 5 (Hollow Keeper, Ratchet Run, Wellward, Pocket Palaeo): each gains a title card, a P/Esc pause overlay with header Pause and Sound buttons and auto-pause when the tab hides, synthesised sound effects, callouts, particle bursts and screen shake, plus a coached first stage that reads live state.
