How the stream works

Select a ground tile, then cut it down or fill it up by one height step. Cut soil goes into your bucket; filling spends it. The bucket holds four scoops. You cannot change rocks, the spring or root beds. Return all borrowed soil to the ground before finishing.

Water always goes to the lowest neighbouring ground that is strictly lower. Equal lowest neighbours split it evenly. It never crosses a flat step or climbs uphill. A hollow with no lower exit traps water. Root beds absorb arriving water. Each bed must receive its labelled share; spare water at one bed does not help the other.

One portable low weir: select a ground tile and the edge to its neighbour. Place the weir to block water across that edge; water then seeks the next lowest open route. Both ground heights must be at most one step apart. Placing, moving or removing it costs one action but no soil. There is only one weir: moving it removes the old barrier. If later digging makes the height gap greater than one, water overtops it until you reshape the ground or relocate it. Dashed amber edges mean overtopped. Source, root beds and rocks cannot anchor it. Undo restores both the barrier and soil.

One treadle pump: select its inlet and an adjacent outlet. It carries all arriving water along that edge, across a flat step or up at most one height. IN marks the inlet and OUT the outlet. Both ends must be ordinary ground. Placement, relocation and removal each cost one action, no soil. It carries water over a weir on its edge. If reshaping raises the outlet more than one above the inlet, the pump stops and normal downhill flow resumes. Water that circles back into its own route is counted as trapped, not duplicated. Undo restores the pump.

Release water as often as you like: a dry bed does not destroy your layout. Reshape and try again. Preview is optional and costs nothing. Undo restores soil; retry preserves records. Finish a garden to unlock the next and try using fewer earthworks. There is no timer.

Keyboard: native Tab/Enter/Space for controls. On the page or terrain, C cuts, F fills, P releases water and Z undoes. Arrow keys move the selected plot. Shortcuts never override selectors or other buttons.
