The mill handbook

There is no real-time clock. Set the water route, gate opening, gear and optional hopper load, then run one stroke. Each mark is part of one tidal cycle. Finish the commission before the final mark.

The bypass moves water in either direction toward the sea level. The wheel only releases stored water to a lower sea. Wheel energy equals water released × the starting height difference; the flywheel stores up to 32. Unused energy loses two per stroke, or one with oiled bearings.

Reduction, direct and overdrive turn the same flywheel energy into different stone speeds and torques. Each sack needs both its speed range and enough torque. A wrong speed or weak gear damages a load; three damaged loads end the commission. Successful loads spend energy. The hopper empties after each stroke.

The next-stroke preview shows the exact water, speed, torque and load outcome before you act. Brake sheds up to four energy after the wheel turns, before the hopper opens. Undo restores the previous stroke and its settings, including after a damaged load or an exhausted tide; completed deliveries stay final.

The counterweight holds up to eight energy without friction. Choose a transfer limit of one to six: smaller transfers keep the remaining energy available in its original store. The limit stays selected, while Bank/Release resets after a stroke. Existing journals default to six. Bank transfers up to the selected limit from the existing flywheel before this stroke’s water arrives; Release transfers up to the selected limit back before the flywheel’s 32-energy limit, brake and hopper. Banking can stall a load; releasing can scorch it or spill energy. Transfer resets to Hold after each stroke. Restart empties both stores; undo and saved journals preserve them.

The governor trims excessive speed automatically, but cannot fix weak torque or slow stones. Earned tools and records remain after a restart. All controls work with Tab, arrow keys and Enter, or touch.
